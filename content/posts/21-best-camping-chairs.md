A good camp chair is where you'll spend most of your evening: by the fire, at the lake, or around the picnic table. Camp chairs have come a long way from the basic folding "quad" chair, and there's now a style for almost every kind of camper.

## Main styles of camping chair

### Quad (folding) chairs

The classic design folds into a long bag. Coleman, Kelty, ALPS Mountaineering (whose King Kong chair is known for its extra-wide seat) and many store brands make them.

- **Good for:** car camping, sports games, the backyard
- **Look for:** cup holders, a side pocket or cooler, armrests at a comfortable height, and a weight rating with margin

### Rocking chairs

Rockers like the GCI Outdoor Freestyle Rocker use spring-loaded arms so you can rock on flat ground. They're popular with people who find sitting still in a chair uncomfortable.

- **Good for:** car camping and porches
- **Watch for:** bulk and weight, so they're best kept close to the car

### Ultralight chairs

Helinox popularized this style with the Chair Zero and Chair One, and brands like Big Agnes, NEMO and MARCHWAY offer similar designs. They pack down to the size of a water bottle and use shock-corded poles with a fabric seat.

- **Good for:** backpacking, bikepacking and kayak trips
- **Watch for:** a low seat height and small feet that sink into soft ground. Add "ground feet" accessories if you camp on sand.

### Low or beach chairs

These sit low to the ground and suit beaches, concerts with height limits and lounging by the fire.

### Loveseats and double chairs

Two-person "duo" chairs are big and heavy, but couples love them for stargazing.

### Heated and padded chairs

Chairs with a battery-heated seat and back, like those from GOBI Heat, extend your season into cold evenings. They need a USB power bank or a power station.

### Chairs with canopies

Models such as the Kelsyus canopy chair have a built-in shade that's useful at sunny sports fields and beaches.

## What to check before you buy

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Weight capacity | Choose a chair rated comfortably above your body weight for durability |
| Seat height | Higher seats are easier to get out of, while low seats are cozier by the fire |
| Packed size | Measure your trunk or pack, especially for rockers and loveseats |
| Back height | A tall back gives head support, while a short one saves weight |
| Fabric | Mesh is cooler in summer; solid polyester is warmer and more durable |
| Feet | Wide feet stay put on grass and sand |
| Setup | Quad chairs open in seconds, while ultralight chairs take a minute to assemble |

## Matching chair to camper

- **Family car camping:** a sturdy quad chair with a cup holder for each person, plus one rocker for whoever does the cooking.
- **Backpackers:** an ultralight chair if you value comfort at camp more than the extra pound or so it adds; otherwise, a sit pad.
- **Overlanders and RVers:** a tall, padded or heated chair you can leave set up all weekend.
- **Bad backs or knees:** choose a higher seat with firm armrests to push up from.

## Care tips

Let the fabric dry before you bag the chair to avoid mildew. Wipe sand out of the joints on ultralight chairs, since grit wears the hubs. Check rivets and stitching at the start of every season.
