Boondocking (also called dry camping or dispersed camping) means parking your RV or van somewhere without electricity, water or sewer hookups. Often that's on public land, where camping may be free for a set number of days. The reward is quiet, space and views no campground can match. The challenge is that you have to bring everything with you.

## Power

Your house batteries run lights, the water pump, the furnace fan and your devices. Without shore power you'll need a way to recharge.

- **Lithium (LiFePO4) house batteries.** You can use far more of their capacity than lead-acid, and they're lighter and longer-lasting.
- **Solar panels.** Roof-mounted panels charge passively all day. Portable panels can be placed in the sun while the RV stays in the shade.
- **Inverter.** Converts battery DC to AC for standard outlets. A pure sine wave unit is safest for electronics.
- **Battery monitor.** A shunt-based monitor tells you exactly how much energy you have left.
- **Portable power station.** A simple plug-and-play alternative for smaller rigs and vans. See our [power station buying guide](/best-portable-power-station-for-camping).
- **Generator (optional).** A quiet inverter generator is a backup for cloudy stretches. Respect quiet hours and local rules.

## Water

Water usually runs out first. Stretch it with:

- Portable water containers or a collapsible bladder to refill your fresh tank
- A 12V transfer pump or a gravity-fed hose
- A water filter for questionable sources
- Low-flow shower heads and a habit of "navy showers" (water off while you soap up)
- Rinse-free wipes and a spray bottle for dishes

## Waste

- **Black and gray tanks:** know your capacities and watch the levels.
- **Portable waste tote** to empty tanks without moving camp.
- **Dump station app or directory** to plan stops.
- **Composting toilet** for vans and rigs that stay out a long time.

## Connectivity

- Cell signal booster
- Satellite internet if you work remotely
- Offline maps and campsite directories
- A satellite messenger for emergencies where there's no cell coverage

## Comfort

- Blackout shades and reflective window covers to manage heat
- Propane heater approved for indoor use, with a CO detector and ventilation
- 12V fans
- Outdoor solar lights

## Safety and recovery

- Leveling blocks (dispersed sites are rarely flat)
- Traction boards and a shovel for soft sand or mud
- Tire repair kit and a 12V air compressor
- Plenty of spare fuel and propane
- First aid kit
- Fire extinguisher and a fire-safety plan

## Boondocking etiquette

- Respect stay limits on public land, often 14 days.
- Camp on durable surfaces in spots that have been used before.
- Keep generators off during quiet hours.
- Pack out all trash and dump tanks only at proper stations.
- Leave plenty of space between you and other campers.

## A simple power budget

| Item | Daily use |
|---|---|
| LED lights | 20 to 40 Wh |
| Water pump | 10 to 30 Wh |
| Furnace fan (cold nights) | 200 to 600 Wh |
| 12V fridge | 300 to 600 Wh |
| Laptop and phones | 100 to 200 Wh |

Add your own devices and compare the total to what your solar and battery can supply. Use the [RV camping essentials checklist](/rv-camping-essentials-checklist) for everything else.
