A weekend trip is long enough that a forgotten item hurts, but short enough that overpacking just means more to unload. This checklist is built for the most common trip: two nights at a drive-in campground. Copy it, cross off what doesn't apply, and keep it in your gear bin for next time.

## Shelter

- Tent with poles, stakes and rainfly (set it up at home once if it's new)
- Footprint or ground tarp a little smaller than the tent floor
- Mallet or a flat rock for stakes
- A few spare stakes and some cord for guylines
- Small brush and dustpan to sweep the tent before packing

## Sleep system

Most bad nights outdoors come from the ground, not the air. Pack for the coldest night in the forecast, not the average.

- Sleeping bag rated at least 10°F (5°C) below the expected low
- Sleeping pad or cot, plus a repair kit if it's inflatable
- Pillow (a real one is fine at a car campground)
- Warm hat and dry socks reserved only for sleeping
- Earplugs and an eye mask

## Camp kitchen

- Stove and enough fuel for every meal plus one spare canister
- Lighter and a backup (matches in a zip bag)
- Pot, pan, and a kettle if you drink coffee or tea
- Plates, bowls, mugs and utensils for each person
- Cooking utensils: spatula, tongs, large spoon, sharp knife, small cutting board
- Cooler with ice or frozen water bottles
- Water jug or bottles (plan on about 1 gallon / 4 liters per person per day for drinking and cooking)
- Biodegradable soap, sponge, collapsible wash basin and a drying towel
- Trash and recycling bags

### Simple weekend meal plan

| Meal | Night 1 | Day 2 | Day 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | | Eggs, bacon, coffee | Oatmeal and fruit |
| Lunch | | Wraps and snacks | Leftovers on the road |
| Dinner | Pre-made chili, reheated | Foil packet veggies and sausages | |

Prepping and freezing the first night's dinner at home means one less job when you arrive late and hungry.

## Clothing

- Moisture-wicking base layers (avoid cotton next to skin if it may get cold or wet)
- Insulating mid-layer like a fleece or light puffy
- Waterproof rain jacket, even if the forecast is clear
- Hiking pants or shorts, plus one pair of comfortable camp pants
- Hiking shoes and camp sandals or slip-ons
- Sun hat and warm beanie
- Extra socks and underwear

## Lighting and power

- Headlamp for every person, with spare batteries
- Lantern for the picnic table
- Power bank for phones
- Optional: a small portable power station if you'll run lights or a fan (see our guide to [choosing a portable power station](/best-portable-power-station-for-camping))

## Health and safety

- First aid kit with blister care, pain relievers, antihistamines and any personal medication
- Sunscreen and lip balm with SPF
- Insect repellent
- Map of the area and campground details saved offline
- Multi-tool or knife
- Fire starter and firewood bought locally (moving firewood spreads pests, and many areas ban it)

## Campsite comfort

- Camp chairs ([how to pick a good one](/21-best-camping-chairs))
- Folding table if the site has no picnic table
- Tarp and cord for rain or shade
- Small rug or mat for the tent entrance
- Cards, a book or a game for downtime

## Before you leave home

1. Check the forecast and fire restrictions for the area.
2. Confirm your reservation and check-in time.
3. Charge headlamps, phones and power banks.
4. Freeze water bottles to use as ice packs that become drinking water.
5. Tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back.

## Packing tips

Pack in clear plastic bins by category: one for the kitchen, one for sleep, one for everything else. Keep the bins stocked between trips, and the next weekend away becomes a matter of loading the car and adding food.

Load the car in reverse order of need. The tent and the cooler come out first, so they go in last.
