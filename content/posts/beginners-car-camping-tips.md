Car camping means driving right up to your campsite. You don't have to carry your gear far, you can bring a real pillow, and if something goes wrong your car is right there. It's the best way to find out how much you enjoy camping before buying a lot of specialized gear.

## Planning

### 1. Start close to home
Choose a campground within a couple of hours' drive. If the weather turns or you forget something important, it's easy to head home.

### 2. Book a site with facilities
For your first trip, pick a developed campground with toilets, potable water and a picnic table. Look at photos or the campground map and choose a site away from the busy entrance and the toilets.

### 3. Check the rules
Look up quiet hours, fire rules, pet policies and check-in times. Many campgrounds ban outside firewood to protect forests from pests.

### 4. Watch the forecast
Plan for the low temperature, not the high. Nights can be much colder than you expect, especially in the mountains or desert.

## Gear

### 5. Borrow or rent before you buy
Friends, outdoor stores and rental services can lend you a tent, pads and a stove. You'll learn what you like.

### 6. Prioritize sleep
A comfortable night makes or breaks a trip. Bring a thick pad or air mattress, a warm sleeping bag and your pillow from home. Read our [camping bed guide](/best-camping-beds-2023) to compare options.

### 7. Use what's already in your kitchen
You don't need special camp cookware at first. A pan, a pot, a spatula and a sharp knife from home will do. The one item you'll need to buy or borrow is a camp stove.

### 8. Bring more light than you think
Each person needs a headlamp, plus a lantern for the table. It gets dark fast once the sun sets.

## Packing the car

### 9. Use bins
Clear plastic storage bins keep things organized and protect gear from rain. Label them: kitchen, sleep, clothes, extras.

### 10. Pack the tent last
The tent goes up first, so it should be easy to reach. Keep the cooler accessible too.

### 11. Keep the essentials up front
A small bag with snacks, water, sunscreen, rain jackets and the campground confirmation should ride in the cabin.

## At camp

### 12. Arrive with daylight to spare
Setting up a tent in the dark for the first time is no fun. Aim to arrive at least two hours before sunset.

### 13. Set up in the right order
Pick a flat, high spot for the tent. Pitch the tent, then set up sleeping gear inside, then the kitchen. With beds ready, you can relax.

### 14. Store food properly
Keep food and scented items in your car or in a bear box if the campground has them, never in your tent. Clean up after every meal.

### 15. Leave no trace
Pack out all trash, use designated fire rings, and leave the site cleaner than you found it.

## Sample first-trip kitchen kit

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Two-burner stove and fuel | Bring a spare canister |
| Lighter and matches | Keep in a zip bag |
| Pot and frying pan | From home is fine |
| Plates, bowls, mugs, cutlery | One set per person |
| Cooler and ice | Freeze water bottles to use as ice |
| Water jug | About a gallon per person per day |
| Wash basin, soap and towel | Biodegradable soap |
| Trash bags | Pack it out |
