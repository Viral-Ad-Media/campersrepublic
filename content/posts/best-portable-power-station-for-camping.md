A portable power station is a big rechargeable battery with outlets. It's quiet, fume-free and safe to use at a campsite where generators aren't allowed. It can charge phones and cameras, run lights and a fan, power a CPAP machine or a 12V fridge, and even brew coffee if it's large enough. The challenge is buying the right size.

## The two numbers that matter

**Capacity (watt-hours, Wh)** is how much energy is stored. A 500 Wh unit can, in theory, run a 50 W device for 10 hours.

**Output (watts, W)** is how much power it can deliver at once. A 300 W inverter can't run a 1,000 W kettle, no matter how big the battery is.

In practice you'll get around 80 to 90% of the rated capacity because of conversion losses, so build that margin into your planning.

## Sizing worksheet

List what you'll run, then multiply watts by hours per day:

| Device | Typical watts | Hours/day | Wh/day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone charging (x2) | 10 | 2 | 40 |
| LED string lights | 10 | 5 | 50 |
| Small fan | 15 | 8 | 120 |
| CPAP (no humidifier) | 30 to 60 | 8 | 240 to 480 |
| 12V compressor fridge | 40 to 60 (cycles on and off) | about 8 running | 320 to 480 |
| Laptop | 50 | 2 | 100 |

Add up the column, multiply by the number of days between recharges, and add about 20%. Check the real numbers on your devices' labels; these are rough examples.

### Rough size guide

- **Under 300 Wh:** phones, lights and a fan for a weekend
- **500 to 1,000 Wh:** adds a CPAP or a 12V fridge for a night or two
- **1,000 to 2,000 Wh:** fridge, CPAP and electronics for a long weekend, plus short bursts of a coffee maker or induction plate
- **2,000 Wh and up:** RV and van setups, or running appliances for longer periods

## Features to compare

- **Battery chemistry.** LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) batteries last thousands of charge cycles and are considered very stable. Older NMC units are lighter but wear out sooner. For a camping unit you'll keep for years, LiFePO4 is worth it.
- **Inverter type.** Look for a pure sine wave inverter; it's safer for sensitive electronics and CPAP machines.
- **Surge rating.** Appliances with motors briefly draw more power when they start.
- **Ports.** Count AC outlets, USB-C (with high wattage for laptops), USB-A and 12V car sockets.
- **Charging options.** Wall charging speed, car charging, and the maximum solar input in watts and volts.
- **Pass-through charging.** Lets you charge the unit while it powers your devices.
- **Weight.** Units above roughly 1,000 Wh can get heavy, so check for wheels or sturdy handles.
- **App and display.** Remaining runtime estimates help you budget power.

## Solar charging

Pairing a power station with folding solar panels lets you stay out longer. Some basics:

- Panel output on a real day is often well below the rated wattage, especially with clouds or a poor angle.
- Make sure the panel's voltage is within the power station's input range.
- Angle panels toward the sun and adjust a few times a day.
- In winter or under trees, plan on much less solar input.

## Tips for making the battery last

- Use 12V DC outlets for 12V devices to skip inverter losses.
- Turn off the AC inverter when you aren't using it, since it draws power even when idle.
- Pre-cool your fridge at home on mains power.
- Store the unit at around 50 to 80% charge and top it up every few months.
