Few camping experiences look as magical as a glamping dome lit up at night. Domes are popular at glamping resorts, in backyards and on rental properties. If you're thinking of buying one or booking a stay, here's what you should know.

## Types of glamping dome

### Geodesic domes
Made from a frame of steel or aluminum struts covered with a PVC or canvas skin, these are the domes you see at resorts. They range from compact units to large structures with room for a bathroom.

- **Pros:** very strong in wind and snow when properly anchored, with year-round use possible
- **Cons:** heavy, take time to build, and larger models need a platform and sometimes a permit

### Clear bubble tents
Inflatable PVC domes kept up by a blower fan. Brands like Vevor, Alvantor and many smaller makers sell them.

- **Pros:** an uninterrupted view of the sky, quick setup
- **Cons:** the fan must run continuously, they get very hot in direct sun, and privacy is limited

### Inflatable and garden igloos
Smaller domes with PVC panels over a lightweight frame, often marketed as garden igloos.

- **Pros:** affordable, easy to set up for a party or a backyard getaway
- **Cons:** less durable and not designed for strong wind

## What to look for

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Cover material | Thicker PVC lasts longer and resists UV; canvas breathes better |
| Frame | Galvanized steel is strong; aluminum is lighter and won't rust |
| Wind and snow ratings | Essential for any permanent install |
| Ventilation | Domes trap heat and moisture; look for vents or windows |
| Door | Zip doors are simple; framed doors seal better |
| Anchoring | Platform bolts, ground anchors or water ballast for windy sites |
| Size | Measure for the bed, a heater and room to move |

## Managing heat and condensation

Clear and dark-colored domes become greenhouses in the sun. To stay comfortable:

- Pick a site with afternoon shade
- Use shade covers or inner curtains on the sunny side
- Run a fan or air conditioner in summer
- Add a dehumidifier or crack a vent on cool nights to control condensation
- Use only heaters that are safe for indoor use, with a CO detector

## Making it feel luxurious

- A real bed on a raised frame, with quality linens
- Rugs over a solid floor or deck
- Warm string lights or lanterns
- A telescope or a star chart for clear nights
- Blackout curtains for sleeping past sunrise

## Buying vs booking

If you only want a night or two, booking a dome at a glamping site is easier and cheaper. Buying makes sense for a rental business, a long-term backyard retreat or a seasonal camp, but factor in the platform, permits, heating, insurance and upkeep.
