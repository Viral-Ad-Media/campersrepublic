Trail riding combines exercise, adventure and a healthy dose of fun. A trail bike is a mountain bike designed to climb reasonably well and descend confidently on singletrack. If you're new to the sport, here's how to choose one.

## Hardtail vs full suspension

**Hardtail** bikes have a suspension fork at the front and a rigid rear end.

- Lighter, simpler and cheaper
- Less maintenance
- Teach good line choice and handling
- Rougher ride on rocky or rooty descents

**Full-suspension** bikes have both front and rear suspension.

- More control, comfort and traction on rough terrain
- Heavier and more expensive
- More parts to maintain

For beginners on a budget, a quality hardtail often beats a cheap full-suspension bike.

## Wheel size

| Size | Ride feel |
|---|---|
| 29-inch | Rolls over obstacles easily, carries speed, stable |
| 27.5-inch | Playful and quick to turn, often better for smaller riders |
| Mixed ("mullet") | 29 front for rollover, 27.5 rear for agility |

## Key components

- **Suspension travel.** Trail bikes usually have 120 to 150 mm. More travel handles bigger hits but adds weight.
- **Brakes.** Hydraulic disc brakes offer strong, consistent stopping and are worth prioritizing.
- **Drivetrain.** Modern 1x (one front chainring) drivetrains are simpler and easier to use than 2x or 3x setups.
- **Dropper post.** Lowers your saddle with a lever for descents. A big confidence boost.
- **Tires.** Wider tires with aggressive tread give more grip; tubeless setups reduce flats.

## Frame materials

- **Aluminum:** affordable, stiff and durable, the most common choice
- **Carbon fiber:** lighter and more expensive
- **Steel:** smooth ride and very durable, but heavier

## Budget bikes and department-store brands

Brands such as Mongoose, Huffy, Royce Union, Gravity, Outroad, Vilano and Tommaso sell affordable mountain-style bikes online and in big stores. They can be fine for paved paths, gravel and easy dirt trails. For regular singletrack riding, look for:

- An air or coil suspension fork with adjustable rebound, not a basic spring fork
- Hydraulic (or at least good mechanical) disc brakes
- A frame sized to you rather than "one size fits most"

If your budget is limited, a used bike from a reputable bike brand, checked by a mechanic, often gives more performance for the money.

## Sizing

Bike sizes vary by brand, so check the manufacturer's chart using your height and inseam. Better still, test-ride. A good fit lets you:

- Stand over the top tube with some clearance
- Reach the bars with a slight bend in your elbows
- Extend your leg almost fully at the bottom of the pedal stroke when seated

## Essential gear for trail riding

- Helmet (consider a mountain-specific helmet with more rear coverage)
- Gloves and eye protection
- Hydration pack or bottles
- Multi-tool, pump, spare tube or tubeless plugs
- Padded shorts for longer rides
- Flat pedals and grippy shoes for beginners

## Riding tips for beginners

1. Look ahead to where you want to go, not at obstacles.
2. Keep your pedals level and stand up on descents.
3. Use both brakes gently and progressively.
4. Shift before climbs, not halfway up.
5. Start on green-rated trails and work your way up.
